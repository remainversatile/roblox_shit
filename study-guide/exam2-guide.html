Chapter 6

The Normal Distribution

Normal distribution

Continuous, bell-shaped and symmetric, described by μ and σ. Mean = median = mode. Total area under the curve = 1, with .50 on each side of μ.

Standard normal distribution

μ = 0 and σ = 1. A z-score converts any normal value to this scale: the number of standard deviations above (+) or below (−) the mean.

Empirical rule

About 68%, 95% and 99.7% of values fall within 1, 2 and 3σ of μ. For exactly 95%, use 1.96.

Continuous variable

P(X = a) = 0, so P(X < a) = P(X ≤ a).

Formulas

\(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\)z-score (raw data)
\(\mathrm{x}=\mu+(\mathrm{z})(\sigma)\)z-score to a raw score
\(\mu=\mathrm{x}-(\mathrm{z})(\sigma)\)z-score to a population mean

Finding a value (area → value)

Turn the wording into a left area (top 10% → .90, bottom 20% → .20), find that area inside the table, read z, then use x = μ + (z)(σ).

Course example: goldfish lifetimes, μ = 60 days, σ = 20. More than 80 days: z = 1.00, 1 − .8413 = .1587. Between 25 and 45: .2266 − .0401 = .1865. Top 10%: 60 + 1.28(20) = 85.6 days.

Chapter 7

Sampling and Sampling Distributions

Parameter vs. statistic

A parameter describes the population (μ, σ, p). A statistic describes a sample (\(\bar{x}\), s, \(\bar{p}\)).

Sampling distribution

The distribution of a statistic over all possible samples of the same size n. Its standard deviation is the standard error.

Sampling error

The natural difference between a statistic and the parameter from sample to sample. It isn't a mistake.

Random sampling

Simple random, stratified random (sample from every subgroup), systematic (every kth item, k = N/n), cluster (randomly pick some groups).

Nonrandom sampling

Convenience, judgment, quota.

Survey errors

Coverage (selection), nonresponse, measurement, and sampling error.

Sampling distribution of \(\bar{x}\)

\(E\,(\,\bar{x}\,)=\mu_{\bar{X}}=\mu\)Expected value for sampling distribution of \(\bar{x}\)
\(\sigma_{\bar{X}}=\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\)Standard error of the mean (σ known)
\(z_{\bar{x}}=\dfrac{\bar{x}-\mu}{\sigma_{\bar{x}}}\)z-score (sample mean)

Shape: normal for any n if the population is normal. Otherwise approximately normal when n ≥ 30 (Central Limit Theorem).

Sampling distribution of \(\bar{p}\)

\(\bar{p}=\dfrac{x}{n}\)Sample proportion
\(E\,(\,\bar{p}\,)=p.\)Expected value for sampling distribution of \(\bar{p}\)
\(\mathrm{np}\geq 5,\ \text{and}\ \ \mathrm{n}(1-\mathrm{p})\geq 5\)Normality requirements for sampling distribution of \(\bar{p}\)
\(\sigma_{\bar{p}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{p(1-p)}{n}}\)Standard error of the proportion
\(z_{\bar{p}}=\dfrac{\bar{p}-p}{\sigma_{\bar{p}}}\)z-score (sample proportion)

Course examples. Credit cards, μ = $2,864, σ = $775, n = 50: \(\sigma_{\bar{X}}\) = 109.6016, P(\(\bar{x}\) > $3,000) = .1075. Internet use, p = .66, n = 300: \(\sigma_{\bar{p}}\) = .0273, P(within .04 of p) = .8584.

When asked to describe a sampling distribution, state all three: center (expected value), spread (standard error), and shape (normal or not, with the n ≥ 30 or np ≥ 5 check).

Chapter 8

Estimation for Single Populations

Point estimate

One statistic used to estimate a parameter: \(\bar{x}\) for μ, \(\bar{p}\) for p.

Interval estimate (confidence interval)

Point estimate ± margin of error. Width = 2E.

Critical probability

\(\frac{\alpha}{2}\) & \(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\), where α = 1 − confidence level. 95% → .0250 and .9750.

Critical value

The z or t that cuts off those areas: 90% → 1.645, 95% → 1.96, 98% → 2.33, 99% → 2.576.

t distribution

Used when σ is unknown. Bell-shaped with heavier tails than z. Degrees of freedom df = n − 1.

Minimum sample size

Smallest n that gives the desired margin of error E. Always round up.

Formulas

\(\mu=\bar{x}\pm\left(Z_{\alpha/2}\right)\left(\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\)CI estimate for μ (σ known)
\(S_{\bar{X}}=\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\)Standard error of the mean (σ unknown)
\(\mu=\bar{x}\pm\left(t_{a/2}\right)\left(\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\)CI estimate for μ (σ unknown)
\(p=\bar{p}\pm\left(Z_{\alpha/2}\right)\sqrt{\dfrac{\bar{p}\left(1-\bar{p}\right)}{\mathrm{n}}}\)CI estimate for p
\(\mathrm{n}=\left(\dfrac{(z_{\alpha/2})\sigma}{E}\right)^{2}\)Sample size for interval estimate of μ
\(n=\dfrac{\left(z_{\alpha/2}\right)^{2}p^{*}\left(1-p^{*}\right)}{E^{2}}\)Sample size for interval estimate of p
  • z or t: σ given → z. Only s given → t with df = n − 1, even when n is 30 or more.
  • Reading the t-table: row = df, column = upper-tail area α/2. Example: df = 17, α/2 = .05 → t = 1.740.
  • p*: the pilot-study estimate. With no pilot, use p* = .50.
  • Interpretation: “We are 95% confident that the population mean (or proportion) is between L and U.”

Course examples. Verizon, \(\bar{x}\) = 153, σ = 46, n = 85, 95%: (143.22, 162.78). Aerospace managers, \(\bar{x}\) = 13.56, s = 7.8, n = 18, 90%, t = 1.740: (10.36, 16.76). Temporary hires, \(\bar{p}\) = .39, n = 87, 95%: (.2875, .4925). Acme, 98%, E = .03, p* = .40: n = 1447.71 → 1,448.

Across chapters: what gets mixed up

The question is about…Use
One value x (Ch 6)\(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\)
A sample mean \(\bar{x}\) (Ch 7)\(z_{\bar{x}}=\dfrac{\bar{x}-\mu}{\sigma_{\bar{x}}}\), with \(\sigma_{\bar{X}}=\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\)
A sample proportion \(\bar{p}\) (Ch 7)\(z_{\bar{p}}=\dfrac{\bar{p}-p}{\sigma_{\bar{p}}}\), with the known p
Estimating μ or p (Ch 8)Point estimate ± (critical value)(standard error)
  • p vs. \(\bar{p}\): Chapter 7 probabilities use the known p in the standard error. A Chapter 8 CI uses \(\bar{p}\), because p is unknown.
  • “E” means two things: E(\(\bar{x}\)) is an expected value. E alone is the margin of error. “Within 3 percentage points” means E = .03.
  • Middle 95%: of individual values is μ ± 1.96σ. A 95% CI is \(\bar{x}\) ± 1.96σ/√n.
  • Calculator vs. table: the answer key uses table values (z to 2 decimals, 4-place areas, 2.33 for 98%). If your calculator disagrees, enter the table answer.
  • Rounding: follow “round final answer only” or “round all calculations” as each question says. Minimum n always rounds up.

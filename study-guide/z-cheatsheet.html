Z-Score Cheat Sheet

Every z-score on Exam 2 works the same way: (value − center) ÷ spread. What changes is which center and spread you use. The formulas are written exactly as they appear on the course formula sheet.

The three z-scores on your formula sheet

Formula Sheet for Test 2, pages 1–3

Ch 6 · one raw value\[z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\]

Center μ, spread σ. Use it for one person, one fish, one bill.

Ch 7 · a sample mean\[z_{\bar{x}}=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu}{\sigma_{\bar{x}}}\qquad \sigma_{\bar{X}}=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\]

Center μ, spread σ/√n. Use it for the average of n items.

Ch 7 · a sample proportion\[z_{\bar{p}}=\frac{\bar{p}-p}{\sigma_{\bar{p}}}\qquad \sigma_{\bar{p}}=\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}}\]

Center p, spread from the known p. Use it for the fraction in a sample.

Solving the Chapter 6 formula backwards gives the other two lines on the sheet:

z-score to a raw score\[\mathrm{x}=\mu+(\mathrm{z})(\sigma)\]

Find a cutoff from a percentile.

z-score to a population mean\[\mu=\mathrm{x}-(\mathrm{z})(\sigma)\]

Find μ when you know x and its percentile.

Same idea for x̄ cutoffs\[\bar{x}=\mu+(z)\left(\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\]

The cutoff for a sample mean uses the SE in place of σ.

What a z-score tells you

  • z = number of standard deviations from the center. z = 1.50 means 1.5 SDs above. z = −0.75 means three-quarters of an SD below.
  • The sign tells the side. Positive is above the mean, so its left area is > .50. Negative is below, so its left area is < .50. z = 0 is the mean, with area .5000.
  • The size tells how unusual. About 68% of values fall within ±1, 95% within ±2 (exactly ±1.96), and 99.7% within ±3. A z beyond ±3 is very rare.
  • The standard normal has μ = 0 and σ = 1. Converting to z lets one table work for every normal problem.
  • Exact values have zero probability. P(X = a) = 0, so “less than” and “at most” give the same area.

Backward: area → z → value

1. Turn the wording into a LEFT area

  • Bottom 20% → .20
  • Top 10% → 1 − .10 = .90
  • 67.72% are larger than x → .3228
  • x is larger than 86.65% → .8665
  • Middle 95% → .0250 and .9750

2. Find that area inside the table

Read z off the row and column. If the area falls between two cells, take the closest one. If it's exactly halfway, as .9500 is (between .9495 and .9505), use the midpoint: 1.645.

.90 → 1.28 · .20 → −.84 · .8665 → 1.11 · .3228 → −.46

3. Convert back. x = μ + (z)(σ), or μ = x − (z)(σ). A cutoff below the mean has a negative z, so x comes out smaller than μ.

  • Top 10% of goldfish: 60 + (1.28)(20) = 85.6 days
  • Bottom 20%: 60 + (−.84)(20) = 43.2 days
  • Middle 95%: 60 ± (1.96)(20) = 20.80 to 99.20 days
  • Unknown mean: 449 − (1.11)(36) = 409.04

z-score mistakes that cost points

  • Subtracting z-scores instead of areas for “between.” Look up both areas, then subtract.
  • Forgetting “1 −” for “above” or “more than.” The table always gives the left side.
  • Using σ for a sample mean. If the question is about \(\bar{x}\), divide by σ/√n. With σ, the credit-card z would be .18 instead of 1.24.
  • Using p̄ in a Chapter 7 SE. Probability questions use the known p.
  • Looking up the percentage instead of the left area in inverse problems. For the top 10%, use .90.
  • Not rounding z to 2 places before using the table. The key does, and your Canvas answer should too.
  • Percent vs. decimal: .0040 is .40%, not 4%. Enter what the question asks for.
  • Off the table: past z = ±3.49 the left area is about .0000 or 1.0000.
  • Sanity check: a probability above the mean is less than .50, a far tail is tiny, and every answer is between 0 and 1.

On the TI-84

  • Left area of z: normalcdf(-1E99, z, 0, 1). Round z to 2 places first to match the table.
  • Area straight from raw values: normalcdf(lower, upper, μ, σ), with σ/√n for a sample mean.
  • z from a left area: invNorm(area, 0, 1). The calculator gives 1.2816 where the table gives 1.28.
